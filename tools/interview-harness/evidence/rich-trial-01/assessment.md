## Parent evaluation against the candidate fixture

The teamwork answer is grounded in T01: another volunteer's shared role, Amira moving coats and clearing the front, checking the layout together, and improved visibility/access are all supplied facts. It does not invent a measured benefit or claim leadership. The other two answers are explicitly hypothetical; smiling, checking displays and asking a colleague are proposed actions, not unsupported past incidents. No candidate history fabrication was observed in this run.

The feedback is more useful than in the thin-fixture trial. It identifies the candidate's actual contribution as a strength and asks for concrete wording to acknowledge an upset customer's frustration as an improvement. It does not dismiss a known outcome as unknown. Its phrasing “unknown from your answer” is accurate but somewhat mechanical for a human coach. One specific example sentence could be explored during the retry rather than supplied during the interview.

The interviewer completed three relevant questions and a clean debrief with no scores, hiring predictions, mid-interview coaching or invented employer claims. No follow-up was needed for the fairly complete teamwork example. This demonstrates a normal path; it does not exercise the follow-up bound, vague answers, unknowns, stopping early, or retry. Two of three questions were hypothetical, so a later quality comparison should watch whether the interviewer explores enough real experience rather than consistently defaulting to hypothetical scenarios.

The candidate used four sentences for the teamwork answer despite the guide's two-or-three-sentence opening preference. The extra sentence reported a supplied outcome and was relevant; this is a minor response-length deviation, not a grounding failure. It also gave most T01 details upfront, leaving less to elicit in a follow-up. Keep that tendency in mind when designing a separate vague-answer scenario.

Conclusion for this trial: the current interviewer prompt works on a richer, grounded normal case. This was a fixture change with the prompt unchanged; it is not evidence of a prompt improvement. Next useful tests are a deliberately vague first answer using the same rich facts, and the retry phase. No prompt change is warranted solely from this run.
