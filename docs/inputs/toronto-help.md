## One-on-one help, free

### Employment Ontario ✓
- **What:** free one-on-one support from an Employment Ontario counsellor: "resume writing, interview training,
  skills training, job search help".
- **How to reach:** over 700 locations; by appointment, email, phone or live chat.
- **Who for:** people looking for work in Ontario. The local provider decides eligibility; it does not depend on
  immigration status.
- **Source:** https://www.ontario.ca/page/employment-ontario

### ACCES Employment ✓
- **What:** helps job seekers facing employment barriers enter the Canadian job market; "most of our services are
  free". Newcomer-focused.
- **How to reach:** programs and events on its site, by service line.
- **Who for:** job seekers, especially newcomers to Canada, in the Toronto area.
- **Source:** https://accesemployment.ca/

### COSTI Immigrant Services ✓
- **What:** employment and career development programs for newcomers and skilled workers, including online
  services. Interview preparation is part of its programs.
- **Who for:** newcomers and skilled workers.
- **Source:** https://www.costi.org/en/employment (path read at source; confirm the host is `www.costi.org`)

### JVS Toronto ✓
- **What:** a four-day Job Search Workshop for Newcomers, with one-to-one support and a certificate; eight-week
  sector-specific programs with employer connections; programs for internationally trained professionals.
- **Who for:** newcomers.
- **Source:** https://www.jvstoronto.org/find-a-job/newcomer-services/ (path read at source; confirm the host)

## At the public library (free with a Toronto Public Library card)

### Career Coaches in Residence ✓
- **What:** "career and job search help for younger adults. Coaches can help with general career advice, resume and
  cover letters, job search, interview preparation, or personal marketing material for your LinkedIn profile,
  portfolio or website."
- **How to reach:** book an appointment: pick a date in the calendar, complete a consent form, wait for
  confirmation.
- **Who for:** younger adults (the page's own words).
- **Source:** https://tpl.ca/programs-and-classes/featured/career-coaches-in-residence/

### New to Canada: settlement workers ✓
- **What:** "Meet with a settlement worker who can help you get started in Toronto and Canada"; library
  information in over 40 languages; citizenship practice tests; language-learning databases including IELTS
  practice.
- **Who for:** newcomers to Canada.
- **Source:** https://tpl.ca/using-the-library/new-to-canada/

### Job Seeker learning pathway ✓
- **What:** online courses: LinkedIn Learning on networking, job interviews (including video interviews) and resume
  writing, plus a Gale Courses resume course with an instructor, over 24 hours. Certificates available.
- **Who for:** anyone with a library card.
- **Source:** https://tpl.ca/elearning/learning-pathways/job-seeker/

### Career and job search classes ✓
- **What:** the library runs free career and job search sessions (resumes, interviews, using AI in a job search).
  These appear in the Events tab's library tier, so the Help entry is one line that points there.

## Routers: when you do not know where to start

### 211 Ontario ✓
- **What:** a free helpline and website that points you to the right community service, including newcomer
  employment programs in Toronto. "If you do not know which agency you qualify for, 211 answers that in one call."
- **How to reach:** call 2-1-1, or the website.
- **Source:** https://211ontario.ca/ (the newcomer-employment topic page was read, but its URL was not recorded;
  link the home page)

## Government guidance (not a person, but free and official)

### Job Bank: "Preparing for an interview" ✓
- **What:** four steps (planning, interview materials, the interview, after the interview); it advises you to "plan
  and rehearse answers to potential interview questions".
- **Source:** https://www.on.jobbank.gc.ca/findajob/resources/prepare-for-interview

## A line for the Help screen about eligibility

Some free programs check immigration status and some do not. The library and Employment Ontario serve people
regardless of status; programs funded by the federal settlement budget are mostly for permanent residents and
protected persons, and agencies vary. **Suggested copy:** "Some programs check your immigration status and some do
not. If you are not sure, call 211 or ask a library settlement worker."
